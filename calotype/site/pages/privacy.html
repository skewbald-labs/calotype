Privacy notice

Calotype is built to know as little about you as it can. This page says exactly what it keeps, what it logs, and what it never stores.

Last changed 23 November 2026: prepaid packs paid in USDC on Tempo, the "Get a free key" button and its tag, two more daily counts, and the name of the calling software in the logs. Before that, 22 November 2026: sign-in with OAuth, prepaid packs, and daily counts of the steps toward a key.

Who is responsible

Calotype is sold by Dostal Technology, a Texas company, and built and run by Skewbald, an AI agent. Questions about this notice, and requests about your data, go to skewbald@agentmail.to.

In short

  • No account, no name, no email address is needed for a free key. You may add an email address to a key, only to be told when it nears its monthly quota.
  • Calotype stores a hash of your key and counts of what it has done, not the key, not the pages you capture, and not the images.
  • Your IP address is held in memory for at most an hour, only to limit key creation and free tries, and never written down by Calotype.
  • A free try on the home page renders the page you name and shows you the image once. The address and the image are not kept; tries are only counted per day.
  • No cookies, no analytics, no trackers, nothing loaded from other sites.
  • Payment details are handled by Stripe, not by Calotype.

What Calotype stores

Calotype's database (Google Cloud Firestore, on the live service) holds one record per key, with:

  • the SHA-256 hash of the key, never the key itself;
  • the key's plan, and when the key was created;
  • how many screenshots the key has used in each calendar month, and how many successful screenshots it has taken in total;
  • whether and when the key was revoked;
  • for Calotype's own keys, a flag saying so: keys made by its operator rather than through the website, which may also carry a short label, and test keys its operator made through the website;
  • if you reached the site through a link tagged like ?ref=dt-linkedin, that tag. Calotype chooses these tags for its own posts, so they show which post brought a key. They say nothing about you;
  • for a key made with the "Get a free key" button, a tag for where the button was: the tag of the link you followed if it had one, else web, try, mcp-link or api-link. It names a page, not you. The button asks for nothing and sets no cookie;
  • for paid keys only, the Stripe customer id and subscription id, which link the key to its subscription;
  • for keys that bought a prepaid pack, the prepaid balance and one record per purchase: the pack, its size and price, the Stripe payment id, when it was paid, and when it was refunded if it was;
  • for a pack paid in USDC on Tempo, the purchase record holds the transaction hash instead of a Stripe payment id, next to the id of the Stripe payment that records it. The hash is public on the Tempo blockchain, where anyone can see the wallet address that paid, the amount and the time. Calotype also keeps the hash, with no key attached, as a record that the transaction was used, so it cannot pay twice; that record is kept when the key is deleted;
  • only if you gave one, an email address for quota emails, and a record of each quota email queued for the key.
  • for keys made or linked through sign-in with OAuth, the app's name as a tag, such as ref:oauth-claude.

It also keeps the ids of the Stripe webhook events it has handled, so that a repeated event is not applied twice. Those ids say nothing about you.

For the free tries, it keeps one record per day (UTC) with counts only: how many tries there were, how many were rendered, and how many succeeded, failed, were refused, were over the hourly limit or came after the daily limit. No address, host, IP address or image.

To see where people and agents get stuck on the way to a key, it also keeps one record per day (UTC) with counts only: how many times the MCP tool was listed or called without a key, a wrong key was sent, OAuth sign-in was looked for, a key or sign-up page that does not exist was asked for, a browser that opened an API address was shown a help page, a key was refused or made, a key was made with the button, and a key took its first screenshot. No address, key, IP address or browser name.

That is all. There is no name, IP address, address of a captured page or image in the database, and no email address unless you added one.

Quota emails (optional)

What. If you give an email address, when you make a key or later with POST /v1/keys/email, Calotype stores it on that key's record.

Why. Only to tell you when the key reaches 80% and 100% of its monthly quota: at most two emails per key per calendar month, and at most four per address per month across all keys. Nothing else is ever sent to it. It is not used for marketing, not shared and not sold.

What else is kept. For each quota email, a record with the key's internal number and reference, the month, the threshold and the counts, kept with the key's record. Once the email is sent, the record also holds a keyed hash of the address, used only to keep to the four-a-month limit. The record never holds the address itself.

Who processes it. The address is stored in Google Cloud Firestore with the rest of the key's record. The emails are sent by AgentMail from skewbald@agentmail.to, so AgentMail handles the address and the text of each email it sends, and a copy of each sent email stays in that inbox, as sent mail does. The program that sends them runs in Calotype's Google Cloud project; the public service never holds the key to the mail account.

How long. On the key's record, until you remove it, or the key is revoked or deleted. Copies of emails already sent stay in the inbox's sent mail.

How to remove it. Open the link at the end of any quota email; it removes the address at once. Or reply "stop" to the email, or call DELETE /v1/keys/email with the key, or write to skewbald@agentmail.to.

IP addresses

To stop one person from making keys by the hundred, the server counts recent key requests per IP address. It counts free tries per IP address the same way, to hold each address to its hourly limit, and app registrations for sign-in with OAuth. Those counts live in the server's memory only. An address is forgotten at most an hour after its last key request or try, and all of them are gone when the server restarts. Calotype never writes an IP address to its database, its logs or a file. Screenshot calls made with a key do not look at your IP address at all.

Free tries

The box on the home page takes a screenshot with no key. Calotype uses the address you paste to render that page once, sends the image back inside the answer page, and keeps neither the address nor the image. The answer page tells browsers not to cache it.

What is kept: the day's counts described above, and one log line per try, like a screenshot's, with the scheme and host of the page (such as https://example.com) and how the try ended. Your IP address is held in memory for at most an hour, as described above, and is not in the counts or the logs. The form sends the address in the body of the request, not in the request line.

Sign-in with OAuth

Apps such as claude.ai and ChatGPT can connect to Calotype's MCP server by OAuth instead of with a key you copy. This is what that keeps:

  • The app's registration is not stored. Its name and return addresses travel inside the client id the app holds, signed by Calotype. Registrations are counted per IP address in memory, as described below.
  • An app named by a web address (a client metadata document) has that document fetched by Calotype, under the same address rules as a screenshot, and kept in the server's memory for five minutes.
  • When you approve, a free key is made exactly as with the key button, under the same hourly limit, and tagged with the app's name. When you paste a key, Calotype checks it and, if it has no tag yet, tags it the same way. The tag names the app, not you.
  • For about a minute, until the app collects it, Calotype stores one authorization code record: a hash of the code, a hash of the app's client id, the app's return address, the code challenge the app sent, and the key in a form that only the code can unlock. Collecting the code deletes the record. A code that is never collected expires after a minute and is deleted the next time any code is made.
  • No cookie is set by the consent page.
  • Logs name the app by its tag, how the sign-in ended and the key's internal number; never the key, the code or the return address.

The app then holds the key and sends it with each screenshot, as any key holder does. To stop it, remove Calotype from the app. You never see a key made this way, so to have it revoked or its record deleted, write to skewbald@agentmail.to with the app's name and roughly when you approved; Calotype finds the key's number in its logs. Deletion then works as described below.

What Calotype logs

Calotype writes one log line per request, to Google Cloud Logging, with: a random request id, the method and path (without the query string), the status, how long it took, the key's internal number, and for screenshots the scheme and host of the page, such as https://example.com. Each line also names the software that made the request: the first word of its User-Agent header, such as curl or mozilla, and a coarse kind (browser, bot or tool). When an MCP client connects, the line for its first request adds the name and version the client reports about itself. The rest of the User-Agent header is never logged or kept. A screenshot line adds the image format, whether it was full-page, the image's size in bytes, the time taken, the page's HTTP status, how many of the page's requests were blocked, and whether the page hit the download limit. A free try logs a line of the same kind, with how the try ended and no key number. Billing events log the key's number, the plan and Stripe's ids. A pack paid in USDC logs the key's number, the pack and the transaction hash.

Calotype's logs never contain your key, the full address you captured, its path or query, your IP address, your email address or your request headers, apart from the one word of the User-Agent named above. Error messages are scrubbed of keys and full addresses before they are written.

What the hosting platform logs

Calotype runs on Google Cloud Run. Google's platform makes its own record of each request it passes to Calotype, including the full request address with its query string, the caller's IP address and user agent. Calotype's Google Cloud project is set to drop those records before they are stored (a Cloud Logging exclusion, in place since 17 November 2026), so they are not kept. Google's front end still handles the request itself in order to deliver it.

Even so, sending the key in the Authorization header, and using POST /v1/screenshot with a JSON body, keeps the key and the page address out of the request line altogether. The website's own buttons already work this way.

Pages and images

The image is made, sent back in the response, and not kept. Each capture runs in a fresh browser context that is thrown away afterwards. Calotype does not store the address you asked for; only its scheme and host appear in Calotype's logs.

Payments

Payment for paid plans goes through Stripe Checkout, on Stripe's pages. Stripe collects your card and the contact and billing details its checkout asks for, and handles them under Stripe's privacy policy. Calotype never sees your card.

Calotype gives Stripe only the key's internal number and the plan, never the key. From Stripe it stores only the customer id and subscription id described above. What Stripe holds, such as the email address on your receipt, can be seen by the seller in its Stripe account and is used only to run your subscription and answer billing questions, such as refund requests.

A prepaid pack is paid with a payment token that the buyer's wallet, such as Link, issues through Stripe. Calotype passes the token to Stripe once, to take the payment, and does not keep it; it never sees the card behind it. It sends Stripe the key's internal number, the pack and the payment request's id, never the key, and keeps only the purchase record described above.

A pack paid in USDC on Tempo is a transfer on a public blockchain. To check it, Calotype sends the signed transaction (or asks for its receipt) to a node of the Tempo network, run by Tempo Labs unless the server names another. It then gives Stripe the transaction hash, with the key's internal number and the pack, so that Stripe can verify the transfer and pay it out in dollars; Stripe records the paying wallet address under its own policy. Calotype does not store the wallet address itself, though it can be read from the hash on the chain.

Email

Email you send to skewbald@agentmail.to stays in that inbox, which is hosted by AgentMail. Skewbald reads it to answer you. It is not copied into Calotype's database.

Cookies

This site and the API set no cookies, and load no analytics, trackers, fonts or scripts from other sites. Stripe's checkout and billing pages are Stripe's own site and follow Stripe's policies.

Who else sees data

Calotype does not sell or share data for advertising. The only other parties are the services it runs on: Google Cloud (hosting, database and logs), Stripe (payments) and AgentMail (the support inbox, and quota emails if you asked for them), and, for packs paid in USDC, the Tempo network, which is public. Data is disclosed beyond them only where the law requires it.

How long it is kept, and your choices

A key's record is kept for as long as the key exists. There is no automatic deletion yet. A quota-email address is kept only until you remove it, or the key is revoked or deleted.

To have a key's record deleted, write to skewbald@agentmail.to and include the key, which is how Calotype finds its record; the key stops working once the record is gone. For a paid key you can instead write from the email address on your Stripe receipt. Within 7 days of your request, the key's record (with its email address, if it had one), all its monthly usage counts, its prepaid balance and purchase records, and its quota-email records are deleted from Calotype's database for good. A paid key's subscription is cancelled first, so the plan ends and you are not charged again.

Two things are not deleted this way. Stripe keeps its own records of the payments you made, as the law requires of payment records; Calotype no longer holds the ids that link them to the key. And log lines already written, which carry the key's internal number and the name of the software that called, but not the key or anything else about you, are not rewritten; they are removed when the logs' retention period ends.

You can also simply stop using a free key; nothing else about you is held.
