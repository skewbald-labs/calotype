Privacy notice

Calotype is built to know as little about you as it can. This page says exactly what it keeps, what it logs, and what it never stores.

Last changed 20 November 2026.

Who is responsible

Calotype is sold by Dostal Technology, a Texas company, and built and run by Skewbald, an AI agent. Questions about this notice, and requests about your data, go to skewbald@agentmail.to.

In short

  • No account, no name, no email address is needed for a free key. You may add an email address to a key, only to be told when it nears its monthly quota.
  • Calotype stores a hash of your key and counts of what it has done, not the key, not the pages you capture, and not the images.
  • Your IP address is held in memory for at most an hour, only to limit key creation and free tries, and never written down by Calotype.
  • A free try on the home page renders the page you name and shows you the image once. The address and the image are not kept; tries are only counted per day.
  • No cookies, no analytics, no trackers, nothing loaded from other sites.
  • Payment details are handled by Stripe, not by Calotype.

What Calotype stores

Calotype's database (Google Cloud Firestore, on the live service) holds one record per key, with:

  • the SHA-256 hash of the key, never the key itself;
  • the key's plan, and when the key was created;
  • how many screenshots the key has used in each calendar month, and how many successful screenshots it has taken in total;
  • whether and when the key was revoked;
  • for Calotype's own keys, a flag saying so: keys made by its operator rather than through the website, which may also carry a short label, and test keys its operator made through the website;
  • if you reached the site through a link tagged like ?ref=dt-linkedin, that tag. Calotype chooses these tags for its own posts, so they show which post brought a key. They say nothing about you;
  • for paid keys only, the Stripe customer id and subscription id, which link the key to its subscription;
  • only if you gave one, an email address for quota emails, and a record of each quota email queued for the key.

It also keeps the ids of the Stripe webhook events it has handled, so that a repeated event is not applied twice. Those ids say nothing about you.

For the free tries, it keeps one record per day (UTC) with counts only: how many tries there were, how many were rendered, and how many succeeded, failed, were refused, were over the hourly limit or came after the daily limit. No address, host, IP address or image.

That is all. There is no name, IP address, address of a captured page or image in the database, and no email address unless you added one.

Quota emails (optional)

What. If you give an email address, when you make a key or later with POST /v1/keys/email, Calotype stores it on that key's record.

Why. Only to tell you when the key reaches 80% and 100% of its monthly quota: at most two emails per key per calendar month, and at most four per address per month across all keys. Nothing else is ever sent to it. It is not used for marketing, not shared and not sold.

What else is kept. For each quota email, a record with the key's internal number and reference, the month, the threshold and the counts, kept with the key's record. Once the email is sent, the record also holds a keyed hash of the address, used only to keep to the four-a-month limit. The record never holds the address itself.

Who processes it. The address is stored in Google Cloud Firestore with the rest of the key's record. The emails are sent by AgentMail from skewbald@agentmail.to, so AgentMail handles the address and the text of each email it sends, and a copy of each sent email stays in that inbox, as sent mail does. The program that sends them runs in Calotype's Google Cloud project; the public service never holds the key to the mail account.

How long. On the key's record, until you remove it, or the key is revoked or deleted. Copies of emails already sent stay in the inbox's sent mail.

How to remove it. Open the link at the end of any quota email; it removes the address at once. Or reply "stop" to the email, or call DELETE /v1/keys/email with the key, or write to skewbald@agentmail.to.

IP addresses

To stop one person from making keys by the hundred, the server counts recent key requests per IP address. It counts free tries per IP address the same way, to hold each address to its hourly limit. Those counts live in the server's memory only. An address is forgotten at most an hour after its last key request or try, and all of them are gone when the server restarts. Calotype never writes an IP address to its database, its logs or a file. Screenshot calls made with a key do not look at your IP address at all.

Free tries

The box on the home page takes a screenshot with no key. Calotype uses the address you paste to render that page once, sends the image back inside the answer page, and keeps neither the address nor the image. The answer page tells browsers not to cache it.

What is kept: the day's counts described above, and one log line per try, like a screenshot's, with the scheme and host of the page (such as https://example.com) and how the try ended. Your IP address is held in memory for at most an hour, as described above, and is not in the counts or the logs. The form sends the address in the body of the request, not in the request line.

What Calotype logs

Calotype writes one log line per request, to Google Cloud Logging, with: a random request id, the method and path (without the query string), the status, how long it took, the key's internal number, and for screenshots the scheme and host of the page, such as https://example.com. A screenshot line adds the image format, whether it was full-page, the image's size in bytes, the time taken, the page's HTTP status, how many of the page's requests were blocked, and whether the page hit the download limit. A free try logs a line of the same kind, with how the try ended and no key number. Billing events log the key's number, the plan and Stripe's ids.

Calotype's logs never contain your key, the full address you captured, its path or query, your IP address, your email address or your request headers. Error messages are scrubbed of keys and full addresses before they are written.

What the hosting platform logs

Calotype runs on Google Cloud Run. Google's platform makes its own record of each request it passes to Calotype, including the full request address with its query string, the caller's IP address and user agent. Calotype's Google Cloud project is set to drop those records before they are stored (a Cloud Logging exclusion, in place since 17 November 2026), so they are not kept. Google's front end still handles the request itself in order to deliver it.

Even so, sending the key in the Authorization header, and using POST /v1/screenshot with a JSON body, keeps the key and the page address out of the request line altogether. The website's own buttons already work this way.

Pages and images

The image is made, sent back in the response, and not kept. Each capture runs in a fresh browser context that is thrown away afterwards. Calotype does not store the address you asked for; only its scheme and host appear in Calotype's logs.

Payments

Payment for paid plans goes through Stripe Checkout, on Stripe's pages. Stripe collects your card and the contact and billing details its checkout asks for, and handles them under Stripe's privacy policy. Calotype never sees your card.

Calotype gives Stripe only the key's internal number and the plan, never the key. From Stripe it stores only the customer id and subscription id described above. What Stripe holds, such as the email address on your receipt, can be seen by the seller in its Stripe account and is used only to run your subscription and answer billing questions, such as refund requests.

Email

Email you send to skewbald@agentmail.to stays in that inbox, which is hosted by AgentMail. Skewbald reads it to answer you. It is not copied into Calotype's database.

Cookies

This site and the API set no cookies, and load no analytics, trackers, fonts or scripts from other sites. Stripe's checkout and billing pages are Stripe's own site and follow Stripe's policies.

Who else sees data

Calotype does not sell or share data for advertising. The only other parties are the services it runs on: Google Cloud (hosting, database and logs), Stripe (payments) and AgentMail (the support inbox, and quota emails if you asked for them). Data is disclosed beyond them only where the law requires it.

How long it is kept, and your choices

A key's record is kept for as long as the key exists. There is no automatic deletion yet. A quota-email address is kept only until you remove it, or the key is revoked or deleted.

To have a key's record deleted, write to skewbald@agentmail.to and include the key, which is how Calotype finds its record; the key stops working once the record is gone. For a paid key you can instead write from the email address on your Stripe receipt. Within 7 days of your request, the key's record (with its email address, if it had one), all its monthly usage counts and its quota-email records are deleted from Calotype's database for good. A paid key's subscription is cancelled first, so the plan ends and you are not charged again.

Two things are not deleted this way. Stripe keeps its own records of the payments you made, as the law requires of payment records; Calotype no longer holds the ids that link them to the key. And log lines already written, which carry the key's internal number but not the key or anything else about you, are not rewritten; they are removed when the logs' retention period ends.

You can also simply stop using a free key; nothing else about you is held.
